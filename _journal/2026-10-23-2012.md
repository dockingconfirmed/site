>10h of building and troubleshooting

It has been a while since the last update. We accomplished quite a lot but it has been quite stressful. 

On Day 6, we were able to 3D print the mounts using TPU but it had to be remodeled and reprinted overnight because the supports were too thin and some dimensions were off. The solder wires we bought sucked so we bought another one. Then we found a high quality one in the lab whose owner we do not know but we tried it anyway and it worked perfectly. So we planned to use that the next day instead.

On Day 7, the reprint was tested and it fit perfectly. The motors were soldered to the ESC board and the ESP32 C3 was soldered to the FC. The whole thing was assembled. The only thing between us and a flight test is another ground test. That ground test failed. A motor was acting up and was drawing too much current, about 10x as much current as the other ones at the same throttle. We felt some resistance to the rotation so we figured it might be the motor's connections or something inside. So I opened the motor and found nothing. When it was resoldered, the resistance disappeared, so it must have been a short producing the resistance. Again, I tested it and got the same result: a 10x increase in current draw for that motor. It was time to rest.

On Day 8 (today), we did a swap test to determine whether it was the motor's or the ESC board's fault. It turned out to be the ESC board because the motor in question ran perfectly with normal current draw when using another ESC slot. Hence, unfortunately, we had to buy another one and wait it to arrive next week before attempting the flight tests.

As amateur drone builders, failure is the most likely option. And it is why we build prototypes in the first place.

Other than that, Tom also set up the arming switch in the transmitter earlier and the a new and working LiPo battery was tested.
