~10h of building

The first successful flight tests have been completed.

We completed the build on Day 9. The defective ESC board was replaced and all the motors drew the expected amount of current at 5% throttle. After we finished assembling the drone, we performed a number of tests. First, we pushed the overcurrent protection of the power supply to 5 A. I held down the drone and Tom pushed the throttle to reach 5 A. Next, we moved on to testing the battery. We initially used a smoke stopper but had to abandon it because it was only rated for 0.5A. It didn't matter anyway because the drone worked in the bench supply tests. 

Next, we tested if the attitude and throttle commands were being sent correctly from the transmitter and if the correct motors were adjusting. I held the drone at the bottom to feel if the drone was reacting correctly while Tom sent the commands from the RC transmitter. After a bit of finagling in Mission Planner, we got it right. We ended the day with hover tests that unfortunately became flip tests since the drone would flip aggressively even if only throttle commands were sent.

On the next day, we looked for a nice spot to test: grassy and no people in the near vicinity. Again, we started with a quick hover test. And again, the drone just flipped over and broke a propeller in the process. So we troubleshooted for a while and figured out that the drone was basically compensating roll errors incorrectly such that a positive feedback loop was occurring. After some configurations, we did another flight test and the thing flew! In fact, it flew so high that Tom lost control of the drone and it crashed once again, breaking another 2 propellers. With only 1 more extra propeller, we hoped for the best in the next test. 

The next test went really well. The drone was successfully switched to the Altitude Hold state so it held its altitude for a while, then an auto land command was sent, which also worked nicely. But the drone was rotating in the yaw axis uncontrollably. After a bit of thinking, we figured out that it must be the same problem as before with the roll. So after changing a single configuration, it was time for another test.

Finally, an actual stable flight test in all attitudes was demonstrated. Numerous more tests were performed, testing pitch, roll, and yaw, as well as practicing as a drone operator. No more propellers were lost.

Next, we plan to switch to another control mode that is easier to manage, use the ESP32 to transmit telemetry to a ground station, fix the compass issue, as well as perform waypoint navigation.
