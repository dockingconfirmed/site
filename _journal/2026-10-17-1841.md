2h:40m of building

Tom successfully configured the RC to control the motors. The throttle and attitude controls both worked, although, whether the adjustments being made to each motor were correct is still not verified.

We learned a few things about how ArduPilot operates the motors:
1. At idle, when the transmitter throttle stick is set to its lowest, the motors still spin at a certain level of throttle. We're not sure what throttle percent it's running at when it's idle.
2. If idle for a certain amount of time, ArduPilot automatically disarms the system which completely halts the motors.

We also did a propeller fit test and did a quick motor test with a propeller on. We did throttle tests at 5% and 10%. The tests were successful. At 10%, the motor was consuming about 0.1 A of current.

Lastly, I successfully flashed the DroneBridge firmware to ESP32-C3. I was hoping to do more than this but the firmware ran at ESP-IDF's default (near-maximum) transmit power, which is too high for the ESP32-C3 SuperMini. As a result, its Wi-Fi access point was not detectable by other devices. I had to clone the DroneBridge repo and manually lower the transmit power to 8.5 dBm which made the access point detectable.

Next session, we should be able to finish the ESP32 DroneBridge setup, test the RC receiver and GPS mounts, and extend the motor lead wires to fit the F450 frame. If time permits, a quick flight test may be possible. No rush though.
